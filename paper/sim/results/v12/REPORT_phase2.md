## The v12 gate

I_c(rho) = I(A:B)_rho / (2 log2 min(d_A, d_B)), the quantum mutual information of the two parties over its maximum (for two qubits, I(A:B)/2). G_c = sqrt((I_c(rho1~) + eps)(I_c(rho2~) + eps))/(1 + eps), and Psi_v12 = F G_c C_eff D_hat with everything else as in v11. Exact identity for two qubits: I_v11 = I_c + (I(rho_A) + I(rho_B))/2. The v12 gate is the v11 gate with the marginal (lopsidedness) terms removed; it equals v11 exactly when both marginals are maximally mixed and is smaller otherwise. Reasons for this candidate over the alternatives are in the pre-registration. Implemented as `crt.correlation_informativeness` and `crt.functional(..., gate_kind='v12')` (default 'v11', so every v11 and phase-1 number is unchanged; `test_crt.py` passes).

Correction to the pre-registration text (no rule depends on it): the separable state (|00><00| + |11><11|)/2 has I_c = 1/2, which exceeds I_c(Werner(p)) for p < 0.7476, not 0.749 as written.

## How Psi_v12 relates to accessible-information redundancy

- Spearman correlation between the exact R12_inf (v12 gate) and one observer's accessible information J_H over all 68 Simulation-3 points with primary records: 0.9998. Over the 68 points with symmetric records (all c-maybe points; hazy odd m; hazy even m with the tie fix): 1.0000, with 0 order violations.
- Plainly: **yes.** Wherever the two observers' records are conditionally independent and identically distributed given the pointer value with symmetric errors (every pure c-maybe point, and the hazy points once ties are broken fairly), the exact Psi_v12 is a strictly increasing function of a single number, the Helstrom error P_e, through R12_inf = (1 - h(2 P_e (1 - P_e)))/2, while J_H = 1 - h(P_e). Psi_v12 is therefore a monotone of one observer's accessible information there, and R_delta^Psi is an accessible-information redundancy with a stricter, two-observer threshold (0.9 of the two-observer gate corresponds to J_H of about 0.94 to 0.95 bits, against 0.9 for R_delta^J_H). What Psi adds beyond J_H is (i) that it is computed from finite counts of the observers' records alone, without access to S or to the coupling, and (ii) that it is sensitive to records that are not conditionally independent, where J_H of one observer says nothing about agreement; the scrambled environment is the only such case here, and there both are near zero.
- The primary hazy records at even m (biased by the phase-1 tie rule) are where the correlation is not exactly 1: the v12 gate no longer rewards their bias, but their I(r_A:r_B) is not the symmetric-record function of J_H.

## Post-hoc observations (after results; no verdict changed)

- **The v11 gate on the same runs reproduces the phase-1 failure.** At theta = pi/2, N_E = 12, the v11 gate gives N R12-hat = 127.2 [72.8, 181.6] (m = 2) and 88.0 [68.4, 107.7] (m = 3) with 100 draws, against 27.5 and 27.5 with the v12 gate. The phase-2 pass is the gate change, not the new seeds or the extra draws.
- **The finite-N floor still depends on record bias.** Independent records with exact I_c = 0 give N R12-hat = 27.88 (unbiased), 29.05 (bias 0.4) and 31.94 (bias 0.3, outside the band [25.19, 30.07]). The pre-stated check covered p = 0.4 only (inside). The v12 gate removes the exact (N = inf) bias reward completely and the finite-N one only partly: the sampling covariance of the reconstruction depends on the marginals. In Simulation 2 the mean |bias - 1/2| is 0.004 to 0.026 with a maximum over draws of 0.26, so a few draws sit where the floor is raised; the N_E = 12 means are inside the band anyway. A floor law a_c(bias) would be needed for a test on strongly biased records.
- **Above-floor values at N_E = 8 and 10 are genuine finite-size information, not bias.** For example N_E = 8, m = 3: N R12-hat = 124.3 with exact N I_c = 99.6; N_E = 10, m = 4: 117.1 with N I_c = 96.5. In each row the excess over the floor tracks the exact shared record information (I(r_A:r_B) of about 0.002 to 0.02 bits from a Haar state of 9 to 11 qubits). These configurations were not in the pre-stated test (N_E = 12 only).
- **The fair tie break makes even m no better than m - 1 in the hazy grid.** With R1t, the exact gate at m = 2 equals that at m = 1, and m = 4 equals m = 3 (for example theta = pi/2, h = 0.6: 0.0093 and 0.0382): with a fair coin on the tied subspace the extra qubit adds nothing to a one-bit Helstrom record. The primary records at even m score lower than v11 by exactly their bias term.

## What a referee would attack

1. **The gate was changed after a failure.** The v12 gate was designed in response to phase 1's
   Simulation 2 failure; a pass of Simulation 2 under it is a pass of a repaired instrument, not a
   confirmation of the original one. Phase 1 stays reported as a failure of the v11 gate on this task.
2. **It changes Psi everywhere, not only here.** The v12 gate is no longer a function of the spectrum:
   Werner(p) and the matched separable control sigma(p) of v11 Section 4.1 now differ (sigma(p) has biased
   marginals). Lemma 4, its corollary, the floor constant (34.3 becomes about 27.6), the V2 failure band and
   P2's record all have to be restated for v12. It is still not an entanglement witness (separable
   (|00><00| + |11><11|)/2 scores 1/2).
3. **Psi_v12 is, on these models, a monotone of accessible information.** R_delta^Psi carries no information
   about objectivity that J_H of one observer does not, beyond a stricter threshold. Its claim to novelty must
   rest on being estimable from the observers' records alone at finite N, with a known floor.
4. **The record is still a modelling choice.** Helstrom measurements need the coupling, the pointer basis and
   collective measurements on up to 6 qubits. A local, per-qubit majority vote would give weaker records and
   different R_delta^Psi. The tie rule (primary) versus the fair tie break (secondary) changes the hazy
   numbers at even m.
5. **Classical records cap I_c at 1/2.** Psi_v12 for perfect classical agreement is 0.2375 (r_inf x D_hat x 1/2),
   so the gate's range is half used; and C_eff = 1/2 throughout (two merged estimators) carries no
   information about the records, as in v11.
6. **Thresholds are not calibrated to each other.** 0.9 of the two-observer gate and 0.9 H_S of MI are different
   demands; onset ratios depend on that pairing and on the finite-N reference (secondary column).
7. **The floor is bias-dependent at finite N.** Independent records at bias 0.3 give a_c = 31.9 against
   27.9 unbiased (calibration rows): the v12 gate removes the exact bias reward but not the finite-N one.
   A test on strongly biased records needs a floor law a_c(bias); this was not pre-stated beyond p = 0.4.
8. **Finite statistics.** One N (1e4); B = 200 (S1, S3) and 100 (S2), not 1000; 40 / 30 runs per point; 100 Haar
   draws. The floor band is +/- 8 to 10 percent around a calibrated constant, as in V2; biased independent
   records have their own finite-N floor (calibration rows above).
9. **Regularization is not product-preserving.** rho~ = (1 - delta_reg) rho + delta_reg I/4 of an exactly
   independent record state carries a small shared information: I_c(rho~) is 0 for unbiased product states
   but up to 2.4e-6 for the pure product |00> (7e-9 at bias 0.4 against a constant record). Negligible
   against the floor (a_c/N = 2.8e-3 at N = 1e4), but the exact "eps floor" of L3 holds only before
   regularization.
10. **Scope.** All environments are qubits with one coupling angle; no structured, bosonic or spin-chain
   environment, no multi-level S; the discord gap was searched only in the pre-registered grid.
