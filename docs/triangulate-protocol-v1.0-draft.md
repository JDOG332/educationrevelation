# Triangulate: Pre-Registered Protocol

**Does Ψ rank AI reviewers' error detection better than majority vote and standard agreement statistics?**

Protocol version 1.0 (draft), October 2026. Companion to *Convergent Recognition Theory* v11.0 (J. M. Sellers).

**Status: draft, not yet frozen.** The protocol is frozen when its SHA-256 hash, together with the hash of the sealed answer key (Section 1.4), is published (Zenodo or OSF), before the first API call. After that point, any change is logged as a deviation (Section 9).

## Part I. Triangulate

### 0. What is being tested, and what is not

The CRT paper defines Ψ = F_gated · C_eff · D̂ for quantum tomography. Its structure has three parts:

1. agreement between independent reconstructions of one referent, gated so that shared ignorance does not count;
2. consensus across different methods, with redundant methods penalized;
3. a bounded noise weight.

Triangulate asks whether that *structure*, carried over to a classical setting, ranks which AI-flagged claims in a document are real errors better than (i) majority vote and (ii) the standard agreement statistics, Fleiss' κ and Krippendorff's α.

Triangulate is **not** a test of the paper's weak claim (V1, V2, P2, P3). A pass does not confirm those, and a failure does not falsify them. It tests the motivating sentence of the paper's introduction: that similar cross-check structures appear in many inference settings and that this combination is useful in them.

**Hypotheses (pre-registered):**

- **H1 (absolute; Jeff's bar).** Claim-level AUROC of Ψ against ground truth is ≥ 0.80, **and** at least 7 of the 10 traps are flagged at the pre-registered threshold.
- **H2 (comparative; the review's prediction).** The AUROC of Ψ exceeds that of each primary baseline (B1 majority vote, B3 Fleiss-weighted vote, B4 α-weighted vote), with a 95% paired-bootstrap CI on the difference that excludes 0, after Holm correction across the three comparisons.
- **Logged regardless of outcome:** the invisible-error fraction f_inv (Section 5.4), the softmax diagnostics (n_active per fold), and the ablations (Section 4).

### 1. Item set

#### 1.1 Items

There are **22 items** (Jeff's notes call them "questions"). Each item is a short document of 150–300 words, segmented in advance into numbered claims (one sentence or one equation each), typically 8–12 claims per item. The plan assumes about 220 claims in total.

**Base text sources**, chosen so that no model under test wrote them:

- passages from CRT v8.7 itself;
- textbook-level physics, math and statistics paragraphs written by Jeff;
- short public-domain expository passages.

No base text is generated by any model in the panel.

**Error seeding.** Errors are seeded by hand by Jeff. Claude or another model may *propose* candidate errors, but a model from the panel must not write the final wording of any item, because that could advantage its own family.

#### 1.2 Composition

| Group | Items | Seeded errors per item | Errors |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean | 4 | 0 (one of these carries 2 decoys) | 0 |
| Single | 6 | 1 | 6 |
| Double | 6 | 2 | 12 |
| Triple | 6 | 3 | 18 |
| **Total** | **22** | | **36** |

The 36 errors are spread across seven types, at least 4 of each: dimensional/unit, arithmetic, logical (e.g., necessary ↔ sufficient reversed), citation (wrong author, year or venue), factual, internal inconsistency (a number in one sentence contradicts another), and notation (a symbol used for two things).

At least two items use real residues from CRT v8.7 that the v11 review found: the dangling "P1", "modulator Q", "Lin [20]", "we we", and the √K factor of 10. These count as seeded errors. The paper's history thus becomes part of the test.

#### 1.3 Traps and decoys

- **Traps (10, all among the 36 errors):** subtle errors designed to read as correct to a fluent reader. Examples: an exponent sign flipped inside an otherwise standard formula; a citation year off by one; a unit with the right magnitude but the wrong dimension; "if" where the source has "only if"; a widely repeated misconception stated confidently; an internal count that differs by one between the abstract and the body. Traps are spread across at least 6 items and at least 5 error types.
- **Decoys (2, in one clean item):** correct claims that look wrong (a counter-intuitive but true result). They are negatives. Their flag rate is reported separately as a check on false positives.

#### 1.4 Ground truth and blinding

The answer key is a CSV with columns `item_id, claim_id, is_error, error_type, is_trap, is_decoy`. It is written before any API call, and its SHA-256 hash is published with the protocol.

Models never see the key, the error count, or the existence of traps. The prompt is identical for all items.

If a model flags an unseeded but genuine error in the base text:

- **Primary analysis:** the frozen key is used, so such a flag counts as a false positive.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Jeff adjudicates these flags blind to which model raised them, and the analysis is rerun with the adjudicated key. Both results are reported.

### 3. Mapping the paper's quantities to Triangulate

The unit of analysis is the **claim** c. For each run (model m, resample r), let x_{m,r,c} ∈ {0, 1} be 1 if the run flags claim c, and let q_{m,r,c} = confidence/100 if flagged, 0 otherwise.

- The **primary** analysis uses the binary flags x.
- A **secondary** analysis uses the confidence-weighted q in place of x everywhere below.

The parameters are the paper's: ε = 10⁻⁸, H_min = 10⁻⁶, η = 10⁻⁶, τ = 1.

#### 3.1 Units: same-family models merged as redundant

The primary analysis works on **units** u, the 5 a-priori families. A unit pools the runs of its member models: the OpenAI unit has 6 runs per claim, every other unit has 3.

This is where redundancy control can affect the *ranking*. The paper's own R = n_unique/n_active is a single number per panel, so it scales every claim's Ψ equally and **cannot change the AUROC**. It is computed and reported (5/6), but by itself it does no work in this test. The paper-faithful unmerged variant (6 units, R = 5/6) is reported as an ablation.

#### 3.2 F_gated: within-unit consistency across resamples, gated so that silence does not count

For runs a, b of unit u on claim c:

```
F(a,b)     = ( √(x_a x_b) + √((1−x_a)(1−x_b)) )²        # Bernoulli (Bhattacharyya) fidelity
I(a)       = x_a                                        # informativeness: does the run assert an error?
G_ε(a,b)   = √((I(a)+ε)(I(b)+ε)) / (1+ε)
F_gated(u,c) = mean over run pairs (a,b) within u of F(a,b) · G_ε(a,b)
```

With binary flags, a pair that both flag scores 1, a pair that disagrees scores 0, and a pair that is both silent scores ε/(1+ε). That last case is the ignorance floor, the analogue of the paper's V2. So "no flags" does not count as agreement, and this falls out of the gate rather than being added by hand.

*Deviation from the paper, stated:* in the paper, I = 1 − S(ρ)/log d, because the uninformative output of tomography is the maximally mixed state. Here the uninformative output of a reviewer is silence, so I is the asserted flag. The secondary analysis uses I = q, with q in place of x in F.

#### 3.3 C_eff: cross-unit agreement, redundancy-controlled

- **Per-unit flag rate:** p̄_{u,c} = mean of x over u's runs. P_{u,c} = Bernoulli(p̄_{u,c}).
- **Weights (paper's rule, cross-fitted):** For a held-out item i, compute on the other 21 items each unit's calibration log-likelihood LL_u = Σ_c [ y_c ln p̃_{u,c} + (1−y_c) ln(1−p̃_{u,c}) ], with p̃ = clip(p̄, 0.02, 0.98), in nats. Then:
  - w_raw = softmax(LL / τ), with τ = 1;
  - active set A = {u : w_raw,u ≥ η};
  - renormalize the weights over A.
- **Consensus:** C₀(c) = 1 − JSD_w({P_{u,c}}_{u∈A}) / (H(w) + H_min), with C₀ = 1 if n_active = 1.
- **Redundancy:** R = n_unique / n_active.
  - *Primary:* computed at model level from the a-priori family partition (5/6).
  - *Secondary, empirical (Algorithm 1 analogue):* δ_m = 95th percentile, over calibration claims, of the JSD between pairs of m's own resamples. Draw an edge m–m′ if the mean between-model JSD over calibration claims is < max(δ_m, δ_m′). n_unique = number of connected components. Report whether the empirical partition matches the family partition.
- **Effective convergence:** C_eff(c) = C₀(c) · R.

**Softmax diagnostic (pre-registered prediction of the paper's Section 3.2 note):** with about 200 calibration claims, the LL differences between units are expected to be tens of nats, so n_active = 1 in many folds. The number of folds with n_active = 1 is reported. When n_active = 1, Ψ reduces to the best single unit's gated consistency times its precision, and the report says so.

A **secondary weighting** with τ = n_cal (the number of calibration claims, i.e., softmax over mean per-claim LL) is pre-registered. It shows whether a tempered softmax keeps C_eff active.

#### 3.4 D̂: precision on a calibration set

For each unit u, on the 21 calibration items (cross-fitted):

```
D_u     = Σ over runs and calibration claims of x · y        # true flags  (signal)
D_ref,u = Σ over runs and calibration claims of x · (1−y)    # false flags (noise)
D̂_u     = D_u / (D_u + D_ref,u)                              # = precision; 0 if the unit never flags
```

This is the paper's Eq. 13 with signal = true flags and noise = false flags, playing the roles of coincidences and accidentals. The per-claim value is the precision of the units that actually flagged c, weighted by w:

```
D̂(c) = Σ_u w_u p̄_{u,c} D̂_u / Σ_u w_u p̄_{u,c}     (D̂(c) = 0 if no unit flagged c: "no signal")
```

#### 3.5 Ψ

```
R₁₂(c) = Σ_u w_u F_gated(u,c)
Ψ(c)   = R₁₂(c) · C_eff(c) · D̂(c)           # ∈ [0, 1)
```

### 4. Baselines and ablations

**Primary baselines** (used in H2):

| ID | Score for claim c | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Majority vote: v(c) = fraction of all 18 runs that flag c | Binary majority (v ≥ 0.5) also reported |
| B3 | Fleiss-weighted vote: v(c) · P_c, where P_c is Fleiss' per-item agreement over the 18 runs | P_c is high both when all flag and when all are silent. B3 shows the shared-silence problem the gate addresses. |
| B4 | α-weighted vote: Σ_m α_m⁺ v_m(c) / Σ_m α_m⁺, where α_m is Krippendorff's α (nominal) between m's flags and the pooled flags of the other models on the 21 calibration items (cross-fitted), and α⁺ = max(α, 0) | |

**Secondary baselines and ablations** (exploratory, no pass/fail):

- **B2:** family-merged vote share (isolates the merge).
- **B5:** precision-weighted vote, Σ_m D̂_m v_m(c) / Σ_m D̂_m. This isolates whether Ψ beats plain precision weighting. It is the most important ablation.
- **A1:** Ψ with F in place of F_gated (no gate).
- **A2:** Ψ without C_eff.
- **A3:** Ψ without D̂.
- **A4:** F_gated alone.
- **A5:** paper-faithful unmerged Ψ (6 units).
- **A6:** τ = n_cal.
- **A7:** confidence-weighted Ψ (q in place of x).

**Descriptive only:** panel-level Fleiss' κ and Krippendorff's α over all claims. These are reliability statistics for a whole panel and cannot rank claims by themselves. That is why B3 and B4 turn them into claim-level scores.

### 5. Endpoints and pre-registered decision rules

#### 5.1 Primary endpoint

The primary endpoint is the claim-level **AUROC of Ψ(c) against is_error**, over all claims of all 22 items. Scores are cross-fitted by leave-one-item-out: weights, D̂_u and α_m for item i come only from the other 21 items. Ties count ½, which matters because many claims score 0.

#### 5.2 Trap rule

A claim is "flagged" by a score s if s(c) > θ_s. Here θ_s is the 95th percentile of s over the non-error claims of the other 21 items (cross-fitted, a nominal false-positive rate of 5%; a tie at θ_s counts as not flagged). The rule is the same for Ψ and for every baseline, and the traps-flagged count is reported for each.

#### 5.3 Decision

| Result | Rule |
|---|---|
| **H1 pass** | AUROC_Ψ ≥ 0.80 (point estimate) **and** traps flagged by Ψ ≥ 7/10. Both are required. The 95% CI (bootstrap over items, 10,000 resamples) is reported alongside. |
| **H1 fail** | Either condition not met. |
| **H2 pass** | For each of B1, B3, B4: ΔAUROC = AUROC_Ψ − AUROC_B > 0, with the 95% paired-bootstrap CI (resampling items, 10,000) excluding 0 after Holm correction. DeLong's test is reported as secondary. |
| **H2 inconclusive** | Point estimates favor Ψ but a CI includes 0. Reported as *inconclusive*, never as a pass. |
| **H2 fail** | Any point estimate ≤ 0. |

**Power, stated in advance.** With about 36 positives and about 184 negatives, the standard error of an AUROC near 0.80 is about 0.05 (Hanley–McNeil), so the 95% CI is about ±0.10. A true AUROC difference of 0.05 between Ψ and majority vote is unlikely to be detected. H2 is therefore expected to be inconclusive unless the effect is large. This is a pilot. A larger replication (for example, 60 items) is the planned next step if H1 passes.

#### 5.4 Invisible-error fraction

f_inv = (number of seeded errors flagged by none of the 18 runs) / 36. Every aggregator ties these errors with the silent negatives, so f_inv caps every score's achievable AUROC. The following are reported:

- f_inv overall and for traps;
- the panel ceiling: the AUROC of an oracle that ranks every visible error first;
- AUROC restricted to visible errors (secondary).

If f_inv > 0.25, the run is labeled **panel-limited**: the reviewers mostly cannot see the errors, and no aggregator can fix that.

### 6. Scoring code (outline)

```python
# triangulate_score.py  — outline; run with: python -I triangulate_score.py runs.jsonl key.csv
import json, itertools, numpy as np, pandas as pd
from sklearn.metrics import roc_auc_score
from statsmodels.stats.inter_rater import fleiss_kappa, aggregate_raters
import krippendorff
from scipy.sparse.csgraph import connected_components

EPS, HMIN, ETA, TAU = 1e-8, 1e-6, 1e-6, 1.0
FAMILY = {"m1":"openai","m2":"openai","m3":"anthropic","m4":"google","m5":"meta","m6":"deepseek"}

def H(p):                                  # Shannon entropy in bits of a probability vector
    p = np.asarray(p, float); p = p[p > 0]; return -(p*np.log2(p)).sum()

def bern(p):  return np.array([p, 1-p])

def jsd_w(P, w):                           # Eq. 8, P: list of distributions, w: weights
    mix = sum(wi*pi for wi, pi in zip(w, P)); return H(mix) - sum(wi*H(pi) for wi, pi in zip(w, P))

def f_gated_pair(a, b):                    # binary or confidence in [0,1]
    F = (np.sqrt(a*b) + np.sqrt((1-a)*(1-b)))**2
    G = np.sqrt((a+EPS)*(b+EPS))/(1+EPS)
    return F*G

def f_gated_unit(runs):                    # runs: 1-D array of x (or q) for one unit, one claim, NaN = missing
    r = runs[~np.isnan(runs)]
    pairs = list(itertools.combinations(r, 2))
    return np.mean([f_gated_pair(a, b) for a, b in pairs]) if pairs else np.nan

def softmax_weights(LL, tau=TAU):          # LL in nats
    z = np.exp((LL - LL.max())/tau); w = z/z.sum()
    active = w >= ETA; w = np.where(active, w, 0); return w/w.sum(), active

def load(runs_path, key_path):
    # runs.jsonl: one line per call {model, item, resample, flags:[{claim,confidence,...}] | null}
    # returns X[model][item] -> array (resamples x claims) of {0,1,NaN}; Q likewise for confidence
    ...

def unit_matrix(X, units):                 # pool runs of member models per unit
    ...

def calib_stats(Xu, key, train_items):     # per unit: p̄ on calibration claims, LL_u (nats), D̂_u (precision)
    LL, Dhat = {}, {}
    for u, M in Xu.items():
        y = np.concatenate([key[i] for i in train_items])
        x = np.concatenate([M[i] for i in train_items], axis=1)        # runs x claims
        pbar = np.nanmean(x, axis=0); pt = np.clip(pbar, .02, .98)
        LL[u] = np.sum(y*np.log(pt) + (1-y)*np.log(1-pt))
        tp = np.nansum(x*y); fp = np.nansum(x*(1-y)); Dhat[u] = tp/(tp+fp) if tp+fp > 0 else 0.0
    return LL, Dhat

def psi_item(Xu, units, w, active, Dhat, R, item):
    n_claims = next(iter(Xu.values()))[item].shape[1]; out = []
    A = [u for u, a in zip(units, active) if a]; wA = np.array([w[units.index(u)] for u in A])
    for c in range(n_claims):
        fg   = np.array([f_gated_unit(Xu[u][item][:, c]) for u in A])
        pbar = np.array([np.nanmean(Xu[u][item][:, c]) for u in A])
        R12  = np.nansum(wA*fg)
        C0   = 1.0 if len(A) == 1 else 1 - jsd_w([bern(p) for p in pbar], wA)/(H(wA)+HMIN)
        num  = np.sum(wA*pbar*np.array([Dhat[u] for u in A])); den = np.sum(wA*pbar)
        Dh   = num/den if den > 0 else 0.0
        out.append(R12 * C0 * R * Dh)
    return np.array(out)

def baselines_item(X, key, train_items, item):
    # B1 vote share over all 18 runs; B3 = vote * Fleiss per-item agreement P_c;
    # B4 = alpha-weighted vote with alpha_m from calibration items; B5 = precision-weighted vote
    ...

def run(runs_path, key_path):
    X, key, items = load(runs_path, key_path)
    units = sorted(set(FAMILY.values()))
    Xu = unit_matrix(X, units)
    R = len(units)/len(FAMILY)                         # 5/6, reported; constant across claims
    scores, diag = {"psi": [], "B1": [], "B3": [], "B4": [], "B5": []}, []
    for i in items:                                    # leave-one-item-out cross-fitting
        train = [j for j in items if j != i]
        LL, Dhat = calib_stats(Xu, key, train)
        w, active = softmax_weights(np.array([LL[u] for u in units]))
        diag.append({"item": i, "n_active": int(active.sum()), "H_w": H(w[w > 0]),
                     "LL_gap_nats": float(np.ptp([LL[u] for u in units]))})
        scores["psi"].append(psi_item(Xu, units, w, active, Dhat, R, i))
        for k, v in baselines_item(X, key, train, i).items(): scores[k].append(v)
    y = np.concatenate([key[i] for i in items])
    auc = {k: roc_auc_score(y, np.concatenate(v)) for k, v in scores.items()}
    # + trap rule (cross-fitted 95th-percentile thresholds), f_inv, panel ceiling,
    # + paired bootstrap over items (10,000) for ΔAUROC with Holm; DeLong secondary
    # + descriptive panel-level fleiss_kappa(aggregate_raters(...)) and krippendorff.alpha(...)
    # + ablations A1–A7 by toggling the factors above
    return auc, diag
```

### 8. What each outcome would mean for the paper

| Outcome | Meaning | What it does to the paper |
|---|---|---|
| H1 pass, H2 pass | First evidence that the gated, redundancy-controlled, noise-weighted structure is useful outside optics, beyond vote counting and κ/α | A short remark may cite the pre-registered result as a *classical instance*. It does not touch V1–P3. A larger replication follows. |
| H1 pass, H2 inconclusive | Ψ ranks well but is not shown to beat the baselines at this size | Claim nothing comparative. Run the 60-item replication. |
| H1 pass, H2 fail | Ψ works but adds nothing over majority vote or κ/α weighting | The paper should not claim an advantage over standard agreement statistics. Related Work stays as written. |
| H1 fail, B5 ≈ Ψ | The signal comes from precision weighting, not from recognition structure | Ψ's extra factors are unsupported in this domain. Say so. |
| n_active = 1 in most folds | The softmax collapse predicted in the paper's Section 3.2 note is replicated in a classical setting | Supports Open Question 7. Whether to revisit τ is a later definition decision, not a v11 change. Report A6 (τ = n_cal) alongside. |
| A1 (no gate) ≈ Ψ | The ignorance gate does no work here | Weakens the "shared ignorance" motivation in this domain only. |
| Panel-limited (f_inv > 0.25) | The reviewers cannot see most errors | No conclusion about Ψ. Change the panel or the items. |
| AUROC pass, traps fail | Ψ finds obvious errors but not subtle ones | Report as is. Traps are the harder and more interesting half. |

In every case the result is published with its data, whatever it shows.

## Part II. The Paper's Own Protocol in Simulation

**Goal:** reproduce Appendix B.1 and the P2 Spearman correlation from released code, report how often the softmax goes one-hot at N = 10⁴, and compare Ψ with simple baselines. This is the step the paper's Section 10.3 says should come before any laboratory run. Jeff or Claude can run it. On a laptop it needs about one to a few hours of compute, dominated by the B = 1000 bootstrap.

### Steps

1. **Environment.** Python 3.11 with `numpy`, `scipy`, `pandas`, `qutip>=5`. Fix and publish one master RNG seed, and derive per-task seeds from it.
2. **POVM.** Build the 4-outcome qubit SIC-POVM (tetrahedral Bloch vectors, E_k = (I + v_k·σ)/4) and the 16-outcome product SIC-POVM E_{jk} = E_j ⊗ E_k. Check that Σ E = I and that the 16 × 16 frame matrix is invertible (informational completeness).
3. **States.**
   - B.1 set: |Φ⁺⟩; Werner ρ(p) = p|Ψ⁻⟩⟨Ψ⁻| + (1−p)I/4 at p = 0.8 and p = 0.2; a "separable mixed" state (B.1 does not say which, so use the diagonal product-basis state with the Werner p = 0.2 spectrum and record that choice); and I/4.
   - P2 grid: p = 0, 0.05, …, 1.
   - P2 separable control: implement the paper's rejection sampler (mixtures of local product states, |ΔTr ρ²| < 10⁻⁴, |ΔS| < 10⁻⁴, PPT check). Note that the diagonal product-basis state diag(λ₁, λ₂, λ₃, λ₄) with the Werner spectrum is separable and matches purity and entropy *exactly*. Use it as a check on the sampler and as the default control.
4. **Data.** For each state and replicate: draw N = 10⁴ counts from the multinomial over the 16 outcomes, and split them into two disjoint batches by random interleaving (≈ 5,000 each). Use 200 replicates per B.1 state and 100 per P2 grid point.
5. **Reconstruction stack** (n = 2 for B.1, as the paper states; n = 3 for the C_eff study):
   - MLE by the iterative RρR algorithm (Hradil / Řeháček), run from two starting seeds;
   - linear inversion with projection to the nearest PSD state (Smolin, Gambetta and Smith 2012);
   - hedged MLE (Blume-Kohout 2010) as the third.

   Apply ρ̃ = (1 − δ_reg)ρ + δ_reg I/4.
6. **F_gated (Eqs. 3–6).** `qutip.fidelity` returns Tr√(√ρ₁ρ₂√ρ₁), *not* its square, so square it to get Eq. 3. Use `qutip.entropy_vn(rho, base=2)` and d = 4.
7. **C_eff (Eqs. 7–12, Algorithm 1).**
   - Compute Pᵢ(k) = Tr(E_k ρᵢ) on the full dataset.
   - Compute cross-validated multinomial log-likelihoods: 5 folds with a published seed, summed over held-out folds, in nats.
   - Weights: softmax with τ = 1, then η = 10⁻⁶, then renormalize. Compute JSD_w, H(w), C₀.
   - Bootstrap with B = 1000. The protocol uses a single 16-outcome setting, so the "per-setting block" is the whole multinomial; record this. Set δᵢ from the bootstrap.
   - Build the graph 𝒢 and take its components with `scipy.sparse.csgraph.connected_components`, giving n_unique, R and C_eff.
8. **D̂.** B.1 does not state D̂. Report each value two ways: (a) F_gated · C_eff (D̂ excluded), and (b) Ψ with an assumed CAR = 100, i.e., D̂ = 100/101 ≈ 0.990. Record the assumption.
9. **B.1 reproduction.**
   - For each state, report mean ± s.d. of Ψ at nominal (ε = 10⁻⁸, δ_reg = 10⁻⁶, η = 10⁻⁶).
   - On the same data (common random numbers), sweep ε ∈ {10⁻¹⁰, …, 10⁻⁶} and δ_reg ∈ {10⁻⁸, …, 10⁻⁴}, and report Max |ΔΨ|.
   - Compare with the table, and with the analytic gate bound I(ρ): 0.576 for p = 0.8 and 0.039 for p = 0.2. The v11 note expects the reproduction to fall near these bounds rather than at 0.71 and 0.28.
   - Separately report Max |ΔΨ| for the Bell state at δ_reg = 10⁻⁴. The v11 note estimates about 6 × 10⁻⁴ for rank-deficient MLE output.
10. **P2 Spearman.**
    - Compute Spearman ρ_s between Ψ and concurrence over the p grid (all replicates and grid means), with p-value.
    - Compare the entangled and matched-separable families at each matched spectrum (Mann–Whitney on replicates, Holm across grid points).
    - **Pre-stated expectation:** ρ_s > 0.95 is expected almost regardless of the score, because both quantities are monotone in p above 1/3 (paper Section 10.6). The informative output is the family comparison.
11. **Softmax collapse.** At N = 10⁴, for every state and replicate, record n_active, the LL gap between leader and runner-up (nats), H(w) and C_eff. Report the fraction of runs with n_active = 1, overall and per state. Repeat at N = 10³ and 10⁵ to show the trend. Report the τ = n-normalized variant as a secondary.
12. **Baselines for P2 (Open Question 8).** Using the same entangled vs. separable separation statistic, compare Ψ against:
    - (i) plain F between the batch reconstructions;
    - (ii) purity Tr ρ̂²;
    - (iii) I(ρ̂) alone;
    - (iv) the bootstrap spread (s.d.) of F.

    **Falsifier for usefulness:** Ψ separates the families no better than F alone or purity alone.
